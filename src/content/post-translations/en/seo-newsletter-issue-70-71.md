<h2 id="toc-1">[Update] A New Helpful Content Update and Link Spam Update</h2>

<p>In the final month of 2022, Google announced two algorithm updates: the “<a href="https://twitter.com/googlesearchc/status/1600171901669605376">Helpful Content Update</a>,” launched on December 5, and the “<a href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/december-22-link-spam-update">Link Spam Update</a>,” launched on December 14.</p>

<p>The second update is especially noteworthy, as many people in the community reported significant volatility. This was the first time Google truly used SpamBrain to target link spam. The announcement also said that, in addition to trying to identify “sites buying links,” Google would begin detecting “sites used for the purpose of passing outgoing links” and would “nullify” or “neutralize” those links. In other words, Google did not mention penalties, and there would be no notification in GSC, but a site could still see traffic volatility when links previously considered beneficial lost their value.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img src="./wp-content/uploads/2023/01/image.png" alt="Google Search Liaison announcement about the December 2022 link spam update" class="wp-image-4089" width="377" height="521" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><a href="https://twitter.com/searchliaison/status/1603125001309167616">Twitter link</a></figcaption></figure>

<p>Links: <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/advanced/guidelines/report-spam">Report spam to Google</a> / <a href="https://www.seroundtable.com/google-december-2022-link-spam-update-34574.html">Search Engine Roundtable coverage</a></p>

<h2 id="toc-2">[Update] Infinite Scrolling Comes to Desktop SERPs</h2>

<p>In late December 2022, desktop search results also began using infinite scrolling, allowing users to scroll through as many as six pages. It initially rolled out to English-language search in the United States, but I expect it to reach other regions over time, just as the mobile version did after <a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-43.html#toc-1">launching in October 2021</a>.</p>

<p>Google also <a href="https://twitter.com/adsliaison/status/1599892906835222528">confirmed</a> that infinite scrolling would affect ads in the same way it did on mobile: users would see ads after scrolling to the next page, while fewer ads would appear at the bottom of the page.</p>

<h2 id="toc-3">[News] E-A-T Becomes E-E-A-T with Experience</h2>

<p>E-A-T—Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness—is an important set of criteria Google uses to evaluate webpages in its Search Quality Rater Guidelines. In mid-December, Google added Experience as another criterion, extending the concept from E-A-T to E-E-A-T.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/12/image-14-1024x533.png" alt="Diagram showing trust at the center of Google's E-E-A-T framework" class="wp-image-4083" width="507" height="264" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>The Search Quality Rater Guidelines say that Trust is the most important part of E-E-A-T</figcaption></figure>

<p>There are two main reasons for adding Experience. First, firsthand experience is extremely valuable in ordinary product reviews, even when the content is not necessarily expert or authoritative. Second, with YMYL content involving money or health, someone searching for information about cancer might want to learn how other people coped with changes in their lives after cancer (experience), rather than read advice from medical experts (expertise).</p>

<p>Links: <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/google-raters-guidelines-e-e-a-t">Official announcement</a> / <a href="https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf">Search Quality Rater Guidelines</a></p>

<h2 id="toc-4">[Official] Google Launches the Search Status Dashboard</h2>

<p>Google launched a Search Status Dashboard that divides Google Search into three parts: Crawling, Indexing, and Serving. When any of these areas experiences a widespread system issue, it will appear on the dashboard, giving everyone a place to check whether the problem is affecting more than just them.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/12/image-15-1024x394.png" alt="Google Search Status Dashboard with crawling, indexing, and serving status" class="wp-image-4084" width="540" height="208" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>

<p>The documentation for using the dashboard also says that when an issue is detected, Google's Search Relations and Engineering teams respond in four stages: “Detection and initial response,” “Investigation,” “Follow up,” and “Mitigation or fix.” If a problem comes up in the future, you can refer back to the definitions of each stage.</p>

<p>Links: <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/status-dashboard">Official announcement</a> / <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/help/status-dashboard">Official documentation</a> / <a href="https://status.search.google.com/">Search Status Dashboard</a> / <a href="https://status.search.google.com/summary">Search status history</a></p>

<h2 id="toc-5">[Official] Google Search's Visual Elements Gallery</h2>

<p>Web results on Google Search can take many different forms. To help people better define these elements, their names, and the ways to control them, Google created a visual elements gallery covering 22 common appearance features.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/12/image-12.png" alt="Annotated diagram naming the visual elements of a Google text result" class="wp-image-4080" width="595" height="263" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>The different parts of a text result, shown in English</figcaption></figure>

<p>“Title link” is a term that emerged only after Google began changing webpage titles in <a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-42.html#toc-7">2021</a>. The documentation also has a Chinese version. Some of its translations are a little odd, but at least it provides official terminology to follow for elements such as breadcrumbs, favicons, and sitelinks.</p>

<p>Links: <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/visual-elements">Official announcement</a> / <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/visual-elements-gallery">Visual elements gallery documentation</a></p>

<h2 id="toc-7">[Tool] Screaming Frog v18 Update </h2>

<p>Screaming Frog reached version 18, and this major update brought three improvements that I find especially useful. The first was GA4 integration, which lets you retrieve GA4 data while crawling a site—an indispensable update for 2023. The second was PDF crawling. Broken links in PDFs are often overlooked because ordinary tools do not crawl PDF files. Finally, there were in-app updates. In the past, every minor update required downloading the app again from the website; from then on, you could skip that step and update it directly in the background.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/12/image-16.png" alt="Screaming Frog announcement listing features in version 18" class="wp-image-4087" width="407" height="639" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><a href="https://twitter.com/screamingfrog/status/1599684424018571264">Screaming Frog announcement</a></figcaption></figure>
