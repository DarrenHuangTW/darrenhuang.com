<h2 id="toc-3">[Good article] Possible reasons for seeing a lot of impressions but few clicks in GSC</h2>
<p>Brodie’s article is about the doubts that many SEOs have. Occasionally, we will see a large number of exposures to a certain webpage in GSC, but the click-through rate is surprisingly low. This is actually related to how GSC records data. Every link on the search results, not just the general web page results, will be recorded in GSC. Therefore, these large amounts of exposure may come from the results of extraordinary web pages on the SERP.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 60 – 2022.07.01~2022.07.15" class="wp-image-3725" decoding="async" height="116" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/07/image-7-1024x251.png" width="474"/><figcaption>sitelinks will also be recorded on impressions and clicks</figcaption></figure>

<h2 id="toc-7">[Experiment] Removing structured data of low-scoring product reviews caused an increase in traffic</h2>
<p>This test on the SEMRush website is very interesting. Many people will pursue structured data and rich results, but if the product's rating is not good, what is the reason why putting structured data will make its click-through rate lower?</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 60 – 2022.07.01~2022.07.15" class="wp-image-3722" decoding="async" height="399" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/07/image-5-1024x851.png" width="480"/><figcaption>SERP appearance comparison before and after testing</figcaption></figure>
<p>The tested e-commerce website has more than 500,000 products. They tested all products with a rating of three or less (out of five).
The<code>aggregateRating</code>tag was removed, and the result was an increase of nearly 21.7% in organic traffic within 21 days. The confidence level of the experiment was as high as 99%.</p>
<p>Personally, I think it is a pity that there are no public ranking changes, but friends who run e-commerce websites may wish to try this strategy.</p>

<h2 id="toc-8">[Good article] Analyze the "pattern" of keywords</h2>
<p>One step when analyzing keywords is classification. The purpose is to classify words with the same concept but different words into a cluster, so as to see which "topic" has a higher search volume, and which replacement words should be used when writing to express the same concept.</p>
<p>However, the classification method introduced in this article is quite different. What they want to classify is the pattern of words used by users when searching, and use this to determine the template for the website's copy title. For example, more people search for<code><em>{location}{room type} for rent</em></code>or<code><em>{room type} for rent {location}</em></code>, based on the search volume of the two templates, determine whether the page title should be "Taipei Suites for Rent" or "Suites for Rent in Taipei".</p>
