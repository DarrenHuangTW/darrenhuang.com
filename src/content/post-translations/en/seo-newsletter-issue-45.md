<h2 id="toc-4">[News] Google Cross-Language Ranking on SERP</h2>
<p>On the search results (SERP),<strong>Google will start to do "cross-language rankings"</strong>", for example, when you use Chinese to search for questions about Thailand, if the information does not have many Chinese website results, Google may translate the Thai website into Chinese and use Chinese titles/descriptions on the SERP. After users click in, they will directly see the content automatically translated into Chinese by Google.</p>

<h2 id="toc-5">[Good article] Website indexing issues and how to improve them</h2>
<p>In the SEO community, we often see people discussing that the indexing speed of their website is very slow, or that the rate of webpages not being indexed is very high. I think this article written by Tomek Rudzki is worth reading, and I recommend it, especially to people with similar problems.</p>
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 45 – 2021.11.16~2021.11.30" class="wp-image-3182" decoding="async" height="278" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2021/11/%E7%B4%A2%E5%BC%95%E6%B6%B5%E8%93%8B%E7%AF%84%E5%9C%8D%E5%A0%B1%E8%A1%A8-1024x475.jpg" width="600"/><figcaption>Index issues and quantities listed on Index Coverage Report in GSC</figcaption></figure></div>
<p>Usually when a website grows to a certain scale, there will be crawl budget and quality problems. You will see in GSC that not all URLs are included. There are three common indexing problems - "<em>Crawled - Not yet indexed</em>」、「<em>Found - not yet indexed</em>」、「<em>Duplicate pages</em>」。</p>
<p>Google's resources are not unlimited in either the retrieval or indexing stages. Therefore, if a website has indexing problems (webpages that should be included are not included), the SEO's job is to carefully diagnose the technical health of the overall website.</p>

<h2 id="toc-8">[Tip] Use niche market forums to come up with content topics</h2>
<p>If you run a content website or company blog, I believe you will occasionally get stuck when thinking about topics. Forum websites in the same field will be a good way to discover new topics.</p>
<p>For example, if you are running a pet website, you can first find the relevant forum (ex: www.pets.ca), then throw it into an analysis tool such as Ahrefs, and filter out the ranked words that include question-like words such as why, what, where, how... to see which questions the target audience really has.</p>
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 45 – 2021.11.16~2021.11.30" class="wp-image-3179" decoding="async" height="447" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2021/11/%E9%80%8F%E9%81%8E%E8%AB%96%E5%A3%87%E7%99%BC%E6%83%B3%E9%A1%8C%E6%9D%90-1024x763.png" width="600"/><figcaption>Select the themes that perform well on pets.ca as a reference for content ideas</figcaption></figure></div>
<p>There are many ways to come up with content ideas. In addition to discussing your own content, you can also try to analyze your opponent's website, check out forums or Q&amp;A websites in the same industry, or chat with the company's internal customer service department or public relations department. Good ideas will often come out.</p>
<p>link:<a href="https://seonotebook.notion.site/Outranking-Forums-481894a66fc74a64b9ff950ac1600a01">Original text</a></p>
