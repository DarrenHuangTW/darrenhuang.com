<h2 id="toc-1">[News] The old version of GA will expire in July 2023</h2>
<p>The new version of Google Analytics (GA4) is in<a href="https://blog.google/products/marketingplatform/analytics/new-way-unify-app-and-website-measurement-google-analytics/">Launched in 2019</a>, nearly 3 years ago, Google finally announced that it will retire the generally familiar current version of GA (Universal Analytics, GA3).<strong>Starting July 1, 2023, the free version of Universal Analytics will no longer collect data</strong>(360 account can be used until October of the current year). After this,<strong>users will lose access to the current version of GA in as soon as six months</strong>。 </p>

If you need to compare YoY data by then, you must start taking action now. <p>Website analysis is mainly divided into session-based model and event-based model. The change this time is from the former to the latter. I first came into contact with the event-based website analysis tool (Amplitude) when I joined a new company a few years ago. <h2 id="toc-10">[Abstract] Google Search Podcast – De-SEOing</h2>
<figure class="embed embed--spotify"><iframe allow="autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; fullscreen; picture-in-picture" loading="lazy" src="https://open.spotify.com/embed/episode/1xC5f5B4DrmnCAPE27wcbm" title="Spotify episode"></iframe><figcaption><a href="https://open.spotify.com/episode/1xC5f5B4DrmnCAPE27wcbm" rel="noopener noreferrer">View original content on Spotify</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>This episode was hosted by Martin, Gary, and Lizzy. The topic discussed was "De-SEOing". They coined the term themselves, so don't be surprised if you haven't heard of it, but despite this, the concept is still quite interesting.</p>
<p>Lizzy is a writer responsible for Google search documents. She said that a problem she encountered recently is that the traffic from organic searches is often not the target audience of the document. For example, "<a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/advanced/crawling/remove-information">How to delete content on Google
"</a>" This page is intended for the website owner, but because it ranks high in the generic search terms, it attracts many people who "want their personal information to be deleted on other people's web pages", causing a disparity in visitors' expectations.</p>
<p>Most of the time, SEOs believe that the higher the traffic, the better, but if you attract the wrong traffic, it may cause more confusion. In Lizzy's case, the way the group measures content is to ask everyone at the end of the page whether they were helped after reading it. As a result, this KPI was lowered, negative feedback increased, and some people even asked Google to remove this page so as not to mislead.</p>
<p>One way to solve this problem is to use a more precise title and description, and add the customer group targeted by the page in these two places to prevent non-TA people from clicking in by mistake. The natural traffic of this page later successfully dropped from the original 40% to 20%.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 53 – 2022.03.16~2022.03.31" class="wp-image-3451" decoding="async" height="435" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/03/%E7%A7%BB%E9%99%A4%E5%85%A7%E5%AE%B9-1024x435.png" width="1024"/><figcaption>Supplement - This page now also has an additional obvious prompt, allowing users who accidentally barged in to quickly confirm whether they are the target audience.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Gary added that Google is not perfect. When the search terms are too broad, such as "remove content," Google will not know whether the searcher is the content owner or a general passerby, or wants to remove web pages, YouTube content, or community content.</p>
<p>The gap between search intent and web page intent is the source of user dissatisfaction.<strong>Therefore, the so-called deoptimize does not mean to deliberately worsen the performance of the web page, but it means not to blindly pursue big words, but to take a step back to optimize for the right audience.</strong></p>
