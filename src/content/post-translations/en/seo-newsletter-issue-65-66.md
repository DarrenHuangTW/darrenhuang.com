<h2 id="toc-9">[Abstract] Non-HTML files on the web</h2>
<figure class="embed embed--youtube"><div class="embed__frame"><iframe allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen="" loading="lazy" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/2gXO6qpcQXY" title="YouTube video player"></iframe></div><figcaption><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2gXO6qpcQXY" rel="noopener noreferrer">View original video on YouTube</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In this podcast episode Gary and Lizzi discuss non-HTML files in the online world and how Google handles them.</p>
<p>Gary said that Google's mission is to organize the information in the online world in a useful way, so ideally Google would want to index all different file formats, but in practice, resources are limited, so it will only select files that are used by more people, such as PDF, DOCX, etc. Files that are relatively niche, or files that require specific programs to open, may not meet the information that most users are looking for, so Google will not index them.</p>
<p>Gary wrote an article in 2011 about<a href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2011/09/pdfs-in-google-search-results">How Google indexes PDF files</a>, in<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-18.html#toc-10">previous podcast</a>: Google does not index PDF directly, but converts it into HTML internally, and then indexes the content in the HTML. This conversion action is not only used for PDF, but also includes all .doc and other non-HTML text files.</p>
<p>For multimedia files such as pictures and videos, Google uses different indexers, so there is no need to go through the above process of converting to HTML. Gary said a concept that few people know,<strong>Google will not only index images, it must index the web page and then index the images on the web page.</strong>; In other words, as long as an image on the Internet does not appear in HTML or other text files, the image will not be indexed, that is, it will not appear in the search.</p>
