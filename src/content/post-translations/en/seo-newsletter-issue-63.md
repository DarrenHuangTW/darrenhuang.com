<h2 id="toc-2">[Official] GSC report update classification method</h2>
<p>There was a change to the Google Search Console interface a while ago, aiming to allow users to find problems more clearly and quickly. This update will not affect Google's indexing methods or search results.</p>
<p>The main reason why Google made this change is that in the old interface, URLs were marked with a "warning" status in addition to "valid" and "invalid", and many users were confused about the impact of the "warning status" of web pages. (ex: Is a URL marked "warning" included?)</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 63 – 2022.08.16~2022.08.31" class="wp-image-3859" decoding="async" height="349" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/08/image-31.png" width="450"/><figcaption>Comparison before and after revised interface</figcaption></figure>
<p>The new version of the report only dichotomizes URLs or detection items. In the structured data report, it is "valid vs. invalid"; in the index report, it is "included vs. not included"; and items that were originally warnings will be classified under the most appropriate binary classification.</p>
<p>For example,<em>URL blocked by robots.txt but still included</em>, which was classified as "warning" in the past, is classified as "included" in the new interface. After all, the webpage is indeed included, but there are some problems.</p>

<h2 id="toc-7">[News] GSC’s “Specify International Targets” function will be deprecated</h2>
<p>There was one in the old version of Google Search Console<em>international targeting (specified international target)</em>function can tell Google which country your website is targeted at. But recently, Google announced<strong>This feature will be deprecated on 9/22.</strong></p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 63 – 2022.08.16~2022.08.31" class="wp-image-3842" decoding="async" height="501" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/08/image-20.png" width="452"/><figcaption><a href="https://twitter.com/googlesearchc/status/1562369672430997504">official announcement</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>For multi-country and multi-language websites, Hreflang has always been the best practice. The original international targeting has a very limited use, so it is not surprising that it was abandoned.</p>
<p>However, this also means that the hreflang issues that were originally reported in the international targeting interface will disappear. Webmasters no longer have official data to understand whether the hreflang settings are correct.</p>

<h2 id="toc-10">[Abstract] Discussing the accessibility of websites (web accessibility)</h2>
<figure class="embed embed--youtube"><div class="embed__frame"><iframe allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen="" loading="lazy" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/AgWntR_L75Q" title="YouTube video player"></iframe></div><figcaption><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AgWntR_L75Q" rel="noopener noreferrer">View original video on YouTube</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>This episode interviews Danny to talk about Web Accessibility. He is a UX Interaction Designer at Google and has recently joined the Central Accessibility Team within Google. The first part of the podcast was spent talking about Danny's career at Google and how he came into contact with the field of accessibility.</p>
<p>"Accessibility" is not limited to the field of websites, nor is it necessarily only for people with visual impairments. It has many overlaps with the field of UX, including the contrast of font colors, how to make text easier to absorb or skim... and so on.</p>
<p>The tab key is a commonly used method to determine the accessibility of a website. Ideally, an accessible web page should intuitively jump to the next paragraph when the user presses the tab key. In addition, in order to avoid the situation where the menu is too complicated and requires several tabs to see the main content, a common practice is to put a "skip links menu" option on the first tab to help users browse.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 63 – 2022.08.16~2022.08.31" class="wp-image-3840" decoding="async" height="279" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/08/image-19.png" width="532"/><figcaption>When pressing tab for the first time on the web page, skip to main content (<a href="https://webaim.org/techniques/skipnav/">sample web page</a>)</figcaption></figure>
<p>In addition, if you want to evaluate the accessibility of a website, the best way is to use screen readers to browse the website. Recommended tools include Chrome<a href="https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/screen-reader/kgejglhpjiefppelpmljglcjbhoiplfn?hl=en">chromevox plug-in</a>, built-in in Android phone<a href="https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/6283677">talkback</a>or iOS mobile phone<a href="https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/turn-on-and-practice-voiceover-iph3e2e415f/ios">VoiceOver</a></p>
