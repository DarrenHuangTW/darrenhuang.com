<h2 id="toc-3">Chrome v88 several updates</h2>
<p>This Chrome 88 update has several pretty good highlights;</p>
<p>1.) <strong>Blocks HTTP resources on HTTPS pages</strong>— This is especially important for websites whose images are placed through CDN or in subdomains. It is very common to see that only the main website is equipped with HTTPS, but the security certificates of other sub-resources are forgotten, causing Mixed Content problems.</p>
<div class="wp-block-image has-lightbox"><figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 25 – 2021.01.16~2021.01.31" class="wp-image-2531" decoding="async" height="174" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2021/01/mixed-content-chrome-%E8%AD%A6%E5%91%8A-1024x348.png" width="512"/><figcaption>Image source:<a class="rank-math-link" href="https://support.google.com/chrome/a/answer/7679408">Google Chrome revision information</a></figcaption></figure></div>
<p><strong>2.) Less annoying permission requests</strong>— Too many websites now abuse the notification function. They always want to know your location information or want to send push notifications. This update is my favorite! To turn on this feature, go to this URL to turn it on:<a class="rank-math-link">chrome://flags/#permission-chip</a></p>
<div class="wp-block-image has-lightbox"><figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 25 – 2021.01.16~2021.01.31" class="wp-image-2533" decoding="async" height="99" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2021/01/chrome-%E6%8E%A8%E6%92%AD%E9%80%9A%E7%9F%A5.png" width="350"/><figcaption>Example: License request for new version (<a class="rank-math-link" href="https://chromeunboxed.com/whats-new-chrome-88">Image source: Chrome Unboxed</a>)</figcaption></figure></div>
<p><strong>3.) Paging search function</strong>- The ability to search the content on all tabs at once, which is very helpful for people who tend to accidentally open more tabs at work. To enable this feature, go to this URL to open it:<a class="rank-math-link">chrome://flags/#enable-tab-search</a></p>

<h2 id="toc-8">Official Podcast - Search Off the Road EP.12</h2>
<p>This episode of the Podcast features Cherry Prommawin, a member of the Google Search Quality Team responsible for Asia and the host of last year’s Taiwan<a class="rank-math-link" href="https://publishersonair.withgoogle.com/events/search-basics-tw">Google Search Basics Seminar</a>, the person who helped answer questions. The first point of this episode is,<strong>Cherry mentioned that one of the problems Google encounters in Southeast Asia is that in some languages or specific fields, they do not have enough content to pass back and forth to users. After all, there are many languages (or scripts) in the world that are spoken by relatively few people.</strong>, so part of her job is to encourage people to create content.</p>
<p>Gary added.<strong>There is an "Index Selection" process in the indexing stage. They will not spend less resources indexing just because there are few users of a certain language.</strong>, and in fact, Google has an internal mechanism to ensure that this will not happen.</p>
<p>Another interesting point is,<strong>Gary mentioned that Google’s index is actually hierarchical.</strong>. Because the online world is very large, Google's indexing system also requires a considerable amount of space to store, but the vast majority of web pages may not be updated after they are published, and they are not usually searched. Therefore, this type of "files" will be placed in low-cost but slow storage space, such as SSD (Solid State Drive); in contrast, news-type web pages may appear in search results several times per second, and these types of files will be placed in high-storage cost but fast space, such as RAM (Random Access Memory).</p>
