<h2 id="toc-1">[News] GSC adds CNAME verification method</h2>
<p>GSC verification is divided into two types: domain or url-prefix. In the past, domain verification could only be completed using TXT records in DNS. Now it can be completed using CNAME. Personally, I don’t think this change makes much sense, because generally the permissions of TXT and CNAME records are the same, and you can change TXT if you have the permission to change CNAME.</p>

<h2 id="toc-8">[Learn] Fuzzy Matching and its SEO applications</h2>
<p>Fuzzy Matching refers to the use of mathematics to compare two strings, and then express the degree of similarity between them as 0~1, with 1 representing exactly the same. This concept has many uses in SEO, such as:</p>
<ol><li>Compares two columns of URLs and, for each URL in one column, selects the URL in the other column that is most similar to it. It can be used for redirect mapping when the URL changes, or when you have a bunch of 404 URLs and want to quickly determine the appropriate redirect location.</li><li>Compare the content of a bunch of articles and find articles that may have similar content based on the words used in the articles to find possible internal link opportunities.</li><li>Classification keywords, such as coca-cola and coca cola and cocacola, the spellings of these words do not exactly match, but it is easy to judge their familiarity with the help of fuzzy matching.</li></ol>

<p>「<em>website on Pixnet?</em>」、「<em>Should e-commerce choose Shopify or WooCommerce?</em>?」、「<em>Is WordPress SEO friendly?</em>"...Wait a minute, I think everyone must have encountered the problem in this episode. To put it simply and directly (also the answer given by John in the video),<strong>No</strong>, search engines don’t care how the web page is generated or what CMS is used behind it, they will only look at the final results.</p>
<p>Of course, this does not mean that CMS has no impact at all. Web pages produced by a poor CMS may have a lot of redundant code or cover ads that cannot be modified by users, which will affect SEO. In addition to the CMS itself, many additional plug-ins or plug-ins installed may increase the "burden" of the website.</p>
<p>But ultimately, from Google's perspective, these issues have nothing to do with the platform of the CMS itself. They only focus on the source code and web page experience returned by the final server.</p>
