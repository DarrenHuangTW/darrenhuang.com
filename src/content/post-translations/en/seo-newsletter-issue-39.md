<h2 id="toc-1">[SERP title rewritten] Official statement and concept clarification</h2>
<p>Google began to modify the way web page titles are generated on search results pages (SERPs) in mid-August. In other words, the text set by the webmaster in &lt;title&gt; may be different from the page title on the SERP. It may be better, but it may also be worse. in<a href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2021/08/update-to-generating-page-titles">Announcement
In the discussion on</a>and Twitter, there are several key points and concepts worth clarifying:</p>
<ol><li><strong>Rewriting the title is actually not news</strong>, from
Starting from<a href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2012/01/better-page-titles-in-search-results">2012</a>, Google may not necessarily use web pages<code>&lt;title&gt;</code>as SERP title</li><li>Currently there are approximately<strong>80% web page
The SERP title of</strong>is still in HTML<code>&lt;title&gt;</code></li><li>Previously, Google rewrote the title based on the user’s search terms. With the launch of the new system,<strong>Google now usually only uses one title</strong>, a title that best represents the page. (I say "usually" because someone<a href="https://twitter.com/dannysullivan/status/1430916814926614535">noted</a>Mobile phones and computers may use different titles)</li><li>In addition to the original HTML title, the rewritten title may come from h1, header tags, text that looks like a title on the web page, all text on the web page, and even anchor text used by the back link source</li><li><strong>The SERP title of the web page no longer differs based on the search terms.</strong>, this is different from meta description (<a href="https://twitter.com/dannysullivan/status/1430543751999418373">source</a>)</li><li><strong>The rewritten title has nothing to do with the ranking</strong>, being rewritten does not mean that Google ignored your original<code>&lt;title&gt;</code> (<a href="https://twitter.com/glenngabe/status/1427988386401431558">source</a>)</li><li><strong>Rewrite title and<a href="./passage-retrieval-and-seo.html">Fragment information retrieval (passage retrieval)</a>Not relevant</strong> (<a href="https://twitter.com/dannysullivan/status/1428126914791710721">source</a>)</li><li>If the web page changes<code>&lt;title&gt;</code>, Google will review and select the SERP title after seeing it (<a href="https://twitter.com/dannysullivan/status/1430921548102131718">source</a>)</li></ol>

<h2 id="toc-8">[Official Q&amp;A]
Why didn't<code>site:</code>access all my web pages?</h2>
<p>The question John received in this video was "<em>My website has 130 pages, why<code>site:domain.com</code>Only 28 are shown??</em>」</p>
<p>This question should be listed as one of the top 100 most common SEO questions.<strong><code>site:</code>does not mean that the results are complete and should not be used as a debugging method.</strong>. If you want to determine how many web pages of your own web page are included, you should use the GSC<a href="https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7440203">Index Coverage Report</a>; If you want to peek at the opponent's website, I personally recommend using third-party tools such as SEMrush, Ahrefs, and Screaming Frog.</p>

<p>First of all, the improvement of search results is mainly divided into the improvement of UI or ranking.<a href="https://blog.google/products/search/helping-people-and-businesses-learn-how-search-works/#:~:text=there%20have%20been%204%2C500%20such%20improvements%20in%202020%20alone!">4,500 improvements in 2020 alone</a>! Almost every proposal will need to go through one or more evaluations.<strong>The so-called evaluation may be an external experiment (experiment) or an internal rating (rating)</strong>, where the difference is that rating is please<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-14.html#toc-3">quality rater</a>is used to score, and the experiment is evaluated based on actual user responses, that is, the differences in the appearance of some search results that everyone will see and hear from time to time.</p>
