<h2 id="toc-6">Moz Controversial Article - Change of No. 1 Position on SERP</h2>
<p>Dr. Pete from Moz wrote an article, which roughly stated that the first-ranked webpage on the SERP appeared on average 375px below the SERP in 2003. Due to the emergence of various search appearances, its position has become lower and lower. In 2020, they found that it can even appear at a position as low as 2938px."<em>first webpage ranking</em>"。</p>
<p>The title of this article is "How Low Can #1 Go?" (How low can your first position be?), which triggered a war of words on Twitter. Google spokesperson Danny Sullivan believes that the title (and even the content) of this article is to arouse people's sentiment that SEO is becoming more and more difficult to do. Although the web page results have become lower, videos, photos... and other elements should also be considered part of the natural search results.</p>
<p>Dr. Pete believes that although web pages and other components have natural traffic, the data shows that most people still click on the web page results on the SERP, so it will have an impact that the web page results become lower and lower, and this is the research direction of his article.</p>
<blockquote class="embed embed--twitter"><p>Twitter／X Post</p><a href="https://twitter.com/dannysullivan/status/1233832378793066497" rel="noopener noreferrer">View original post on Twitter／X</a></blockquote>
<p>The author thinks Danny is right. SEO should not only care about web page results, but Dr. Pete’s point is not wrong. The more components on the SERP, the more likely the traffic is to go elsewhere, because it is unlikely that a website will occupy all the positions of these components. This article and the views of both sides are worth thinking about for SEO people. Some well-known figures in the SEO field have also expressed their opinions in the discussion thread. You can go and see how foreign folks write!</p>

<h2 id="toc-11">Firefox launches DNS over HTTPS</h2>
<p>This is a progress in network information security. In the past, even with HTTPS, the records of which websites users visited were still exposed because the DNS resolution process was not protected by encryption. Firefox cooperates with Cloudfare to solve the above security considerations so that users' browsing records will not be eavesdropped/modified.</p>
<p>This article has less to do with SEO, but it is very suitable for people who want to know more about network operations and information security to strengthen their knowledge in this area.</p>
