<p>Semrush published a study to discuss user behavior on Google SERP after entering a search word. Research breaks down behaviors on the SERP into five categories:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Organic clicks</strong></li><li><strong>Paid clicks</strong></li><li><strong>Google clicks</strong>: The URL changes, but it is still Google’s URL.<span>and the search term remains unchanged</span>. The possible reason is that the search went from general search to shopping, images, news...etc.</li><li><strong>Google keyword change</strong>: The URL changes, but it is still Google’s URL.<span>But the search term changed</span>, the possible reason is that you have done other searches or clicked Google's reference search terms.</li><li><strong>Other zero clicks</strong>: The remaining behaviors after deducting the above four are mainly people who order nothing.</li></ul>

<h2 id="toc-6">[Technology] Use<code>fetchpriority</code>to optimize LCP</h2>
<p><code>fetchpriority</code>This HTML attribute can tell the browser the fetching priority of a resource. When the browser renders a web page, it will decide which resource should be fetched first based on various factors (ex: file attributes, location...etc), and this attribute can further ensure that resources (such as LCP) that you want to load in advance have a higher order.</p>
<p>Many people may confuse the two, but<code>fetchpriority="high"</code>and<code>rel="preload"</code>is different. The latter helps the browser "discover" the resources to be downloaded early, while the former helps the browser determine which of the resources to be downloaded should be processed first.</p>

<p>This podcast episode is in mid-October
At<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-67.html#toc-5">Brighton SEO</a>'s event, John and Lizzy's public Q&amp;A was recorded. The host compiled the questions of the guests and asked them questions + chatted. If you have time, you can listen to it and feel the atmosphere of foreign conferences. As for the content, most of them are official answers.</p>
<p><strong>Subdomain vs Subfolder</strong>- John said that everyone knows that subdomain will theoretically be regarded as another website, but when he asked Google's search engineers how to answer this question externally, the answer he got was that there is no difference between the two.</p>
<p><strong>Link Building</strong>- John said that he can imagine that the impact of external links will become less and less in the future, especially as Google becomes better and better at knowing what content to give when. From the perspective of crawling, links are still very important. They help us discover new web pages, but the importance of the ranking part will become less and less. In fact, it is much lower than in the past.</p>
<p><strong>Should the Organization’s Schema be placed on every page?</strong>- Lizzi replied that it was not necessary, but it would not matter if she did. Schema is not only a thing of Google or SEO, but it is completely fine for webmasters to use schema attributes that are not supported by Google.</p>
