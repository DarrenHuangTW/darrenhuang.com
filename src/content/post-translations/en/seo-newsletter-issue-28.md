<h2>PageSpeed ​​Insights Get started with HTTP/2</h2>
<p>PageSpeed ​​Insights started using HTTP/2 for requests in March this year. If the website supports HTTP/2, you may notice an improvement in the detection results; if the website does not support it, you will see the possible effects of using HTTP/2 in the results.</p>
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 28 – 2021.03.01~2021.03.15" class="wp-image-2617" decoding="async" height="290" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2021/03/%E9%80%9F%E5%BA%A6%E6%AA%A2%E6%B8%AC%E4%BD%BF%E7%94%A8HTTP2-1024x387.png" width="768"/><figcaption><a class="rank-math-link" href="https://developers.google.com/speed/docs/insights/release_notes#release-notes-for-pagespeed-insights-api-and-pagespeed-insights-ui">Official announcement</a></figcaption></figure></div>
<p>It is worth mentioning that<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-17.html#toc-8">Googlebot has started using HTTP/2 for retrieval in November 2020</a>, so this change only affects the results measured by PageSpeed ​​Insights related tools, and has no impact on SEO or actual user experience.</p>
<p>In terms of speed and user experience, the benefits of HTTP/2 are undoubted. You may wish to check whether your website supports it. If not, you can use the estimated results of the new version of the detection tool to communicate with engineers.</p>

<h2>Test: Removing the "number" from the title reduced organic traffic by 16%</h2>

<h2>Official Video: User-Generated Content</h2>
<p>In this video, Aurora discusses how user-generated content (UGC) can help or hurt a website, as well as some best practices. Basically, user-generated content generally refers to the content that users can submit on your webpage, including comments, messages, pictures, and videos.</p>
<p><strong>Although UGC is a good channel for webmasters to interact with users, when Google evaluates web pages, it may not be able to distinguish whether the content is produced by webmasters or users. The management responsibility of UGC lies with the webmaster</strong>, so careless management leads to too many spam or illegal comments, which will not only cause the website's SEO effectiveness to be damaged and ads to be removed, but more importantly, the brand's reputation and even other users will be harmed.</p>

<h2>Interesting case: Adult websites disappear from rankings on brand terms</h2>
<p>Marie Haynes shares this problem her Agency, a company that sells adult products "<em>Jack and Jill Adult</em>"Since September last year, the website's ranking on its own brand words has disappeared. The website has indeed been included, and there are no serious technical problems. It is not ranked when the SafeSearch function is turned off.</p>
