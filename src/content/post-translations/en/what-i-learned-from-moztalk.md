<h3>Influence Marketing: Building a Brand through Strategic Connections</h3>
<h3 style="text-align:right">                                                                                                     - Kristen Craft</h3>
<h4>Mere Exposure Effect</h4>
<div>Kristen introduced the Mere Exposure Effect at the beginning, which is a psychological term that says that consumers (human beings) will prefer things they are familiar with. This is also the principle behind Product Placement (placement marketing) and Remarketing.<div style="text-align:center"><a style="margin-left:1em;margin-right:1em"><img alt="Illustration for SEO | 2015.11.17 Moztalk participation experience" height="300" width="400"/></a></div>
</div>
<div></div>
<div>Of course, this type of "impression" focuses on quality rather than quantity, and it is best if there is an emotional connection. Consumers may be deeply impressed by the company because they saw a touching advertisement once, or they may have a bad impression of the company because they saw too many advertisements or negative image placement marketing.<h4>Failure Example: Negative Image Product Placement</h4>There are only scenes of Volkswagen being smashed in the 007 movie. Will such scenes really make consumers think "I want one!!"...?<div style="text-align:center"><a style="margin-left:1em;margin-right:1em"><img alt="Illustration for SEO | 2015.11.17 Moztalk participation experience" height="223" width="400"/></a></div>

Although this video did not impress me very much, it can be seen from Kristen's eyes that this video left a deep impression on her.<h4>Good example two: Make full use of the Mere Exposure Effect</h4>Kristen mentioned a very interesting story. She knew a salesperson who spent all day opening the phone book and making calls or sending emails to introduce the company's products. She hoped that the other person would be patient enough to listen to her and be interested in buying her products. Of course I was frustrated with this approach, but she took a small step and greatly improved her sales performance. The same salesperson first made a phone call and left a voice message briefly telling the other party who she was and what she did. Then a few days later she followed up an email with a video of about 30 seconds in it. She greeted the recipient with a smile and told them to contact her if they needed anything.

<h3>Your Brand's Disruptive Idea Is withing Reach - Ronell Smith</h3>The second speech was from Ronell Smith from Moz. This speech didn't have much focus, but it told me all the things I hate about Content Marketing!!! "Content is the king." Many people like to talk about content, content, saying that because of content, they will increase the chance of being found by search engines. Take a look at a few rival articles, then change the title, change the method, plug in a few keywords, condense and rewrite it, and then it will be a new content, completely regardless of the quality of the article. The content is not just a bunch of content. "10 XXX things you must not know", "15 XXX tips", "7 things about XXX"... and so on.<div style="text-align:center"></div>
<div style="text-align:center"><a style="margin-left:1em;margin-right:1em"><img alt="Illustration for SEO | 2015.11.17 Moztalk participation experience" height="240" width="320"/></a></div>A while ago I saw a collaboration between Buzzsumo and Moz (an example of co-marketing!)<a href="https://moz.com/blog/content-shares-and-links-insights-from-analyzing-1-million-articles" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">A study</a>said that they studied 100,000 random articles on the Internet. 50% of the articles had less than two interactions (like+comment+share) on Facebook, and 75% of the articles did not have half an external link. This type of article can only be used to kill time, without any real help.<div style="text-align:center"><a style="margin-left:1em;margin-right:1em"><img alt="Illustration for SEO | 2015.11.17 Moztalk participation experience" height="307" width="400"/></a></div>

<div style="text-align:left">I once read an article saying that the concept of many digital marketers is that the purpose of writing these articles is not to be read by "people", but to be read by search engines to increase the relevance of the company's brand to the topic. I forgot the author's detailed arguments and evidence, and I can't find the article now ~ but it probably means that this method will not work.</div>

<div style="text-align:left">This paragraph seems to be a bit of a complaint article, but I think Ronell, a real Content Strategist, thinks the same as me. Ronell constantly emphasized in his speech that "Content is not the goal, the goal is the goal." We should not write articles for the sake of writing articles. We should first have a GOAL and then write articles.</div>
</div>As for what this GOAL is, it goes back to the basic concept of Marketing. We must first find out the needs of consumers (problems), and then find ways to solve them (answers). Ronell believes that we sometimes focus too much on our own fields, and the content we produce is similar to our competitors, which makes it difficult to stand out in a highly competitive industry. We should take a step back and think about which areas of the entire market are not yet saturated, and which voices have not yet been heard. After identifying this opportunity, we can write an article worth reading, sharing, and citing.<h3>Applying Digital Ethology to Content Strategy - Rhea Drysdale</h3>
</div>
<div>This is my favorite speech of the night! Rhea’s topic was her last speech at MozCon. MozCon is Moz’s largest event every year. Tickets cost around $1,000. It’s a great deal to hear it for free this time.

This speech is somewhat related to the previous one by Ronell. Rhea mentioned that Google is now increasingly able to understand what users are thinking when searching. As SEOs (Sarch Engine Optimizers), when writing articles or creating other content, the most important thing to ask is "WHY?" We need to be able to interpret the true meaning of users when entering search strings.

Example 1: When a weak woman today feels frightened when she sees a giant red ant, she may not Google "Boston Pest Control Company", "Best Pest Control Company"...those search terms that you might try to put into every corner of the title text, but Google...<div style="text-align:center"><a style="margin-left:1em;margin-right:1em"><img alt="Illustration for SEO | 2015.11.17 Moztalk participation experience" height="173" width="400"/></a></div>
<div style="text-align:left"></div>
<div style="text-align:left"></div>
<div style="text-align:left">Example 2: "How big is a eggplant? When a person Googles this question, what do you think he wants to know?" When Rhea asked everyone, someone answered: When you are making eggplant dishes, you need to know whether the pot can be placed, and the answers are endless. But...wrong! Not one of the approximately 60 people at the scene answered correctly. The number one ranking in Google's SERP was for asking how big the baby is, because many mothers would describe the pregnancy of the baby as being like an eggplant.</div>

Human behavior is not easy to study, so Rhea believes that marketers should use front-line Salespeople, communication with customer service, and execution of Focus Group to infer consumer behavior.</div>
