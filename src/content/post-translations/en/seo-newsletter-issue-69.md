<h2 id="toc-7">[Research] JS websites take nearly 9 times longer to be fully crawled by Google</h2>
<p>We all know that Google can crawl web pages generated by JavaScript (JS), but is the crawling situation the same as that of pure HTML web pages? Is there a time difference? Onely, a foreign company, conducted this experiment and the results showed that compared to websites generated by JS, pure HTML websites were nearly 9 times slower to be crawled by Google.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 69 – 2022.11.16~2022.11.30" class="wp-image-4055" decoding="async" height="309" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/12/image-6-1024x576.png" width="549"/><figcaption>Figure: HTML took 36 hours to crawl; JS took 313 hours, a difference of almost 9 times.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The experimental method is roughly as follows. First, two sets of pages are created (<a href="https://birds.one.ly/html/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Pure HTML</a> &amp; <a href="https://birds.one.ly/js/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">JS generated content</a>), starting from the first page of each set, a text link to the second page will be inserted into the content. In this way, it will continue to the seventh page. The only difference is that the content and links of the second set of websites are all generated by JS. The author wants to understand the crawling time of Google for different methods of generating web pages in this way.</p>
<p>This experiment is simple but thoughtful, even though Google has said<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4pOH8Smd0Xs&amp;t=477s" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Rendering of JS can usually be completed in 5 seconds</a>, but this experiment believes that if the website relies on JS to generate content &amp; links, it will greatly reduce the speed of web page crawling, so JS should still consider using technologies such as server side rendering.</p>

<h2 id="toc-8">[Tools] 3 tools to identify whether content is generated by AI</h2>
<p>In the past, many tools and applications for AI-generated content have been introduced (for example:<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-52.html#toc-6">How do writers produce articles in 38 seconds?</a>), this time from a different perspective, I will introduce how to determine whether a piece of content is generated by AI.</p>
<p>The original article was shared by Glenn Gabe. Three AI article identification tools -<a href="https://writer.com/ai-content-detector/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Writer’s AI content detector tool</a>, <a href="https://huggingface.co/openai-detector/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">GPT-2 Output Detector Demo</a>, <a href="http://gltr.io/dist/index.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Giant Language Model Test Room (GLTR.io)</a></p>
<p>The first two tools are very intuitive. Enter a piece of text and press analyze, and a score will pop up representing how likely it is that the content was written by a real person.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 69 – 2022.11.16~2022.11.30" class="wp-image-4052" decoding="async" height="308" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/12/image-4.png" width="543"/><figcaption>Picture: The author took the first paragraph of Glenn Gabe’s own article to test</figcaption></figure>
<p>third<a href="http://gltr.io/dist/index.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">GLTR.io</a>It is more complicated. Although it is also a one-click analysis, comparison details are provided. From the results, you can see that the text appears on the bottom of the net in different colors. Each word is compared to the previous word. If the word is easily predicted based on the previous word, it will be displayed in green. The probability of being predicted will be displayed in green &gt; yellow &gt; red &gt; purple in order to determine the probability that the article was generated by AI. This website also has sample text generated based on different algorithms, as well as real-life articles from the New York Times and other places for testing, which is very fun.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 69 – 2022.11.16~2022.11.30" class="wp-image-4053" decoding="async" height="501" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/12/image-5.png" width="530"/><figcaption>Figure: GLTR interface</figcaption></figure>
<p>The above tools are for reference only. It does not mean that low AI content will lead to good rankings (and vice versa). If you use AI tools to assist content production, remember to manually review it again.</p>

<h2 id="toc-10">[Abstract] Two or three things about Redirect</h2>
<figure class="embed embed--youtube"><div class="embed__frame"><iframe allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen="" loading="lazy" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/tBjTPxz6gdQ?list=PLKoqnv2vTMUMxMs2PdBlDULqybdlPZxrk" title="YouTube video player"></iframe></div><figcaption><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tBjTPxz6gdQ&amp;list=PLKoqnv2vTMUMxMs2PdBlDULqybdlPZxrk" rel="noopener noreferrer">View original video on YouTube</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In this podcast episode, John and Martin talk about Redirect. There are several concepts that are not often heard and are worth taking note of.</p>
<p>1.) Martin mentioned that in addition to the essential conceptual differences, 301/302 will also affect the behavior of browsers in practice, such as<a href="https://makersaid.com/browser-behavior-301-redirects/">Most browsers will cache 301 records</a>。</p>
<p>2.) The difference between 301/302 and 308/307 is that the latter can be used for post request redirection (such as API), but the difference is not important for SEO. If you want permanent redirection, you can use 307 or 308.</p>
<p>3.) If it is just a change of domain name, it is very simple, and Google will soon know what happened; but if it is a complex change such as merging or splitting the website, don't be surprised to see big fluctuations even if the redirection is done well.</p>
<p>4.) The URL detection tool in GSC will display the status of the final URL. Many people have reported that this is quite confusing, and John also admitted that it is indeed the case, especially for those who want to test whether the redirection is successful.</p>
