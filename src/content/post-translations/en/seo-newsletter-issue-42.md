<h2 id="toc-6">[Tip] Google Discover search parameters</h2>
<p>I personally like to use the little trick of search parameters. Simply put, it is to add some parameters to the "website address" of Google SERP to achieve the filtering effect. For example, add after the URL<code><a href="https://www.google.com/search?q=%E6%90%9C%E5%B0%8B%E5%BC%95%E6%93%8E&amp;num=100">num=100</a></code>can increase the number of search results to 100 on one page; add<code><a href="https://www.google.com/search?q=%E6%90%9C%E5%B0%8B%E5%BC%95%E6%93%8E&amp;num=100&amp;lr=lang_zh-TW">lr=lang_zh-TW</a></code>can only run Traditional Chinese results...and so on.</p>
<p>I recently learned something new, that is, Google Discover can also use parameters to simulate web page results in different situations. First, change the browser to mobile mode, and then modify the main three parameters -<code>q</code>(change search term),<code>lr</code>(change language, and<code>tbs=qdr:</code>(change time range).</p>

<h2 id="toc-9">[Concept] Will the case in the URL affect SEO?</h2>
<p>in<a href="./how-search-engines-crawl.html#all-about-urls">search engine crawler capture</a>I have written about similar concepts in this article. Among the following six URLs, which ones are the same in the eyes of search engines, and which ones are different?</p>
<pre class="wp-block-code"><code> A) https://darrenhuang.com/about-darren-huang/ (extra trailing slash)
 B) https://darrenhuang.com/about-darren-huang (starts with http instead of https)
 C) https://darrenhuang.com/ABOUT-DARREN-HUANG (uppercase)
 D) https://darrenhuang.com/about-darren-huang (missing www)
 E) https://darrenhuang.com/about-darren-huang?utm_source=facebook (extra tracking parameter)
 F) https://darrenhuang.com/about-darren-huang.html (後面多了.html的檔名) </code></pre>
<p>has the answer in this article, and in the video below, John explains in more detail how Google interprets it. The answer is "all different." The use of uppercase or lowercase URLs in URLs strictly refers to different web pages, but Google also knows that such situations are usually caused by imperfect server settings rather than intentional human effort. Therefore, Google will try to determine whether it is accidental or intentional.</p>
<p>The job of SEO is to reduce the need for Google to "guess" your website. Regardless of the content or structure, the clearer the better. The video also mentions how to give Google clear signals to avoid ambiguity.</p>

<h2 id="toc-10">[Podcast] Google Search Podcast – Gary and Martin argue over JavaScript</h2>
<p>Gary Illyes and Martin Splitt have always liked to bicker over whether JavaScript is a good thing. This podcast does not include John Mueller, so the two chose this topic for a direct duel, discussing the origin and evolution of JavaScript, its benefits and disadvantages.</p>
<p>I won’t help organize the summary for this podcast, because this topic involves the historical evolution of a language, and requires a rich CS background to organize it concisely. I only have a small amount of research on JS, and I am not qualified for this role. However, this podcast episode roughly talks about JavaScript, which was originally a server-side language (called Mocha at the time). , was later used as a browser language (called LifeScript at the time), so its nature is not actually a language born for today's website design, but it evolved slowly. Due to some historical factors (there are more detailed stories in the podcast), people found that JS is very suitable for being used to do things related to websites, so they developed things like AJAX, In recent years, extended applications such as jQuery have been used to build frameworks for websites using JS as the base, such as React, Angular, and Vue.</p>
<p>Both Martin and Gary were born in the background of developers. Gary believes that JS is too "low-end", the definition is not rigorous enough, and it is prone to unexpected consequences. Martin does not deny this, but he also counters that evolution is like this, and JS has its convenience, although developers often use it in the wrong places very creatively. I would recommend this podcast to people who have some programming skills (even better if it’s JS) or developers. It’s quite interesting for two people to discuss while bickering, but if you have no experience at all, it may be a bit unfamiliar.</p>
