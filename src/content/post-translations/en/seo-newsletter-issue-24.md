<h2 id="toc-2">subtopics was launched in mid-November last year</h2>

<h2 id="toc-4">Gadget - Display Google Trends data directly on search results</h2>

<h2 id="toc-8">What is the difference in the "request indexing" function after the return?</h2>
<p>The "request indexing" function in GSC disappeared for a while at the end of December<a class="rank-math-link" href="https://twitter.com/googlesearchc/status/1341437011618623496">is back</a>. But some places seem different. Foreign netizen Nick LeRoy conducted an analysis and found the following two points:</p>
<p>1. In the past, about 50 URLs could be submitted every day. Now, only 10~12 URLs can be submitted every 24 hours to reach the quota limit.</p>
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 24 – 2021.01.01~2021.01.15" class="wp-image-2462" decoding="async" height="333" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2021/01/%E6%8F%90%E4%BA%A4%E7%B6%B2%E5%9D%80%E9%A1%8D%E5%BA%A6.png" width="617"/><figcaption>The quota for submitting URLs is approximately 12 URLs every 24 hours.</figcaption></figure></div>
<p>2. The speed of submission to the index seems to have slowed down. Among the 12 URLs submitted by the author, only 3 were included within a few minutes, and the others were not included until the next day.</p>
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 24 – 2021.01.01~2021.01.15" class="wp-image-2463" decoding="async" height="140" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2021/01/%E7%B4%A2%E5%BC%95%E9%80%9F%E5%BA%A6-1024x280.png" width="512"/><figcaption>The time difference from submitting a web page to actually indexing it</figcaption></figure></div>

<h2 id="toc-10">Official announcement: Four changes to GSC index coverage report</h2>
<p>These updates are intended to give webmasters a more detailed understanding of possible issues that Google detects when crawling their sites:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Cancel<strong>『</strong>Index exception (crawl anomaly)<strong>』</strong>Category</strong>: This category was canceled because the original explanation was too vague, and instead the error was assigned to a more suitable category.</li><li>Pages that have been submitted and indexed, but blocked by robots.txt, will be reclassified to the report "Indexed, but blocked by robots.txt" (warning). In the past, it would be placed in "Submitted but blocked" (error)</li><li><strong>"Indexed, but no content" (warning)</strong>: The newly added category means that this webpage will appear in the Google index, but for some reason, Google cannot read the content of the webpage.</li><li><strong>Forwarded 404 (soft 404) reporting is more accurate</strong></li></ul>
<p class="has-light-gray-background-color has-background">The original explanation of index anomaly (Crawl Anomaly) in the official document is as follows (it does not give any specific suggestions...) —<br/><br/><strong>There is a retrieval problem with the submitted URL.</strong>: You submitted this page for indexing, but Google encountered an unknown crawl error that cannot be attributed to any other reason. Please try using the URL inspection tool to debug your page.</p>
<p>link:<a class="rank-math-link" href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2021/01/index-coverage-data-improvements">official announcement</a></p>
