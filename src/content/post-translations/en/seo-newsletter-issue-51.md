<h2 id="toc-2">[Discussion] Is Google dead?</h2>
<p>Discussions about Google being in decline or Google being dead appear every once in a while, and there was another article recently:<a href="https://dkb.io/post/google-search-is-dying"><em>Google Search Is Dying</em></a>",exist<a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/technology/comments/st9ri1/google_search_is_dying/">Reddit</a>，<a href="https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30347719">Hacker News</a>,and<a href="https://twitter.com/rsg/status/1493727021914116097">Twitter</a>All major communities are fermenting, and Google search spokesperson Danny Sullivan is also actively discussing with everyone in various discussion threads.</p>
<p>The article mainly says that many people will add reddit after the search word (for example, search "<em>best home office chair reddit</em>"rather than"<em>best home office chair</em>"), specifying that you want to see results on reddit (one of the largest forums in the world), or results from forums such as stack overflow and hacker news, and do not trust the natural ranking given by Google.</p>
<p>This is indeed something that no matter whether you are an SEO or an ordinary user, you will feel it. Advertising aside, most Google search results for product recommendations are occupied by marketing articles. However, users are becoming more and more numb to these articles and prefer more "real" discussion of opinions such as comments and forums.</p>
<p>This point has been discussed many times in e-newsletters in the past. Traces of machine-produced content can be seen everywhere in English. Many of these<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-40.html#toc-8">Website traffic performance</a>Pretty good, but this trend has led to<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-38.html#toc-2">Homogenization of search</a>。<strong>Although machine-produced or SEO-oriented articles are relatively complete in terms of keyword placement and layout compared to forum discussion threads, and the Google search algorithm does not accidentally rank them at the top, they have gradually lost the trust of users.</strong>Regardless of whether Google can rely on EAT in the future or<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-30.html#toc-2">Product Review Algorithm</a>To change this situation, I think marketers should take a step back and examine the purpose of creating content. I highly recommend everyone to read these discussion threads!</p>

<h2 id="toc-3">[Intelligence] Google Pirate Update review</h2>
<p>In 2012, Google<a href="https://search.googleblog.com/2012/08/an-update-to-our-search-algorithms.html">announcement</a>An algorithm used to punish websites that plagiarize content. The algorithm does not have an official name, but the SEO community often refers to it as "<em>Pirate Update</em>" (I guess it may be to be consistent with Panda and Penguin launched at the same time, both starting with "P").</p>
<p>This algorithm mainly targets web pages with a large number of web pages that violate intellectual property rights. However, it was rarely discussed after the announcement until a recent report provided by Google to the U.S. Intellectual Property Office.<a href="https://torrentfreak.com/images/Goog-CO.pdf">file
More information was revealed in</a>. The more interesting points include -</p>
<ol><li>Remove terms related to intellectual property violations in search autocomplete or related terms</li><li>If a website receives a large number of "removal requests", the algorithm will demote it</li><li>It is also difficult to escape punishment by redirecting the punished domain to a new domain</li><li>Specially strengthens infringement of still-in-theaters or prerelease works</li><li>Websites that have been demoted will have an average traffic drop of 89%.</li></ol>

<h2 id="toc-4">[Good article] The reasons and details of the disappearance of Favicon</h2>
<p>A good article written by Glenn Gabe. I thought it was nothing after reading the title, but after reading it I found that I had silently absorbed some little knowledge about favicon! Favicon refers to the website logo seen on the search results. It is generally presented in this way in the source code.<code>&lt;link rel="shortcut icon" href="/path/to/favicon.ico"&gt;</code>, if the favicon provided by the website fails, Google will provide a normal icon on the SERP.</p>
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 51 – 2022.02.16~2022.02.28" class="wp-image-3364" decoding="async" height="485" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/02/%E4%B8%80%E8%88%ACfavicon.jpg" width="322"/><figcaption>Uncustomized favicon</figcaption></figure></div>
<ul><li>Although some SEO staff believe that the disappearance of favicon is related to the quality of the website, he does not think so. The author said that in every situation he has encountered, there is a definite technical reason every time, unless the favicon is an obviously pornographic or easily arousing symbol of hatred.</li><li>The favicon file must be at least 48*48, or a multiple thereof (96*96, 144*144...etc), and cannot be smaller than 48*48 (such as 16*16), nor can it be a non-integer multiple (such as 50*50). If the file is svg, it is not subject to this restriction.</li><li>favicon must be square. If the length and width are inconsistent, the illustration may be deformed or not used.</li><li>Google actually has one<a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/advanced/crawling/overview-google-crawlers#google-favicon">Special crawler for favicon</a>, will ignore the limitations of robots.txt, but this spider will only come in handy at certain times. Generally, when crawling favicon, Google still uses ordinary spiders, so robots.txt cannot block the path of favicon and home page.</li><li>As more and more people now like to switch to night reading mode, the website can actually provide favicons suitable for night mode to cater to different interface themes!</li></ul>

<h2 id="toc-5">[Teaching] GSC + BigQuery + Data Studio</h2>
<p>Google Search Console and Data Studio have been introduced many times, and there should be no need to introduce them in particular. The combination of the two can easily visualize GSC data, such as the one introduced in Issue 39<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-39.html#toc-6">Search Intent Analysis Template</a>. However, a very common problem will be encountered in practical applications, that is, when the amount of data is huge, the processing speed will be significantly slower. Sometimes, changing the date or adding a filter will take several seconds to load.</p>
<p>BigQuery comes in handy at this time. Store GSC data in BigQuery*, and then connect Data Studio to BiqQuery (rather than to GSC). There are two benefits to doing this: (1.) Data Studio's processing speed will be greatly improved, and (2.) By repeating this process regularly, you can bypass the 16-month limit of GSC data storage.</p>

<h2 id="toc-8">[Video] Content removed following EU right to be forgotten request</h2>
<p>"Right to be Forgotten" is an online privacy policy adopted by the European Union in 2014, which allows individuals to request search engines to remove their data. Although individuals in EU countries mainly have this right, as the world becomes more and more privacy-focused, some non-EU countries have also begun to adopt this concept and legislate, including Argentina, the Philippines, Turkey, etc.<a href="https://www.facebook.com/LawyerGuitar/posts/2462288190523259/">As far as I know, there is no similar law in Taiwan</a>, but I still recommend everyone to understand this concept.</p>
<p>Once a request is received, the decision to remove the page is based on five factors - inaccurate, inadequate, irrelevant, excessive, and whether it is in the public interest to keep the page in search results.<strong>The so-called "removal" means that a certain web page does not appear in the search results for a certain word (usually the applicant's name), but the web page may appear on other search words. In addition, the webpage will only be removed in regions protected by this law, and the webpage will still be seen in Google searches in other countries.</strong></p>

<h2 id="toc-9">[Video] Does website server location affect SEO?</h2>
<p>The problem with this episode is "<em>Will moving the location of a website server from one country to another affect SEO?</em>”, John replied, whenever Google sees a server change and does not know the location of the server, Googlebot’s crawling speed will automatically slow down to ensure that the new server can withstand Google’s crawling frequency (slow start).</p>
<p>If the server location is close to the user, it may improve the speed and website experience, thereby affecting Google rankings. Webmasters can also use services such as Content Delivery Network to keep the server as close to the user as possible. Finally, server location has nothing to do with Geo-Targeting. If a website wants to target a certain country, it can provide Google signals through settings in GSC or by using a domain with country attributes (ccTLD).</p>
