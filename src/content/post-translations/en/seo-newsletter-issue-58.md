<h2 id="toc-2">[Research] The correlation between internal links and SEO traffic</h2>
<p>The company Zippy studied 1,800 websites, 520,000 URLs, and 23 million internal links, and compared the clicks of the URLs in GSC to try to see the correlation between internal links and SEO traffic. Although the number of samples observed in this type of research is definitely not comparable to that of Google, some observations are still worthy of note and quotation.</p>
<p>When the number of internal links is in the range of 40 to 44, GSC's click volume is the highest, and then it starts to decrease. This may be because internal links of the navigational or sitewide type represent less value than links that appear within body text.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 58 – 2022.06.01~2022.06.15" class="wp-image-3640" decoding="async" height="556" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/06/%E5%85%A7%E9%83%A8%E9%80%A3%E7%B5%90%E8%88%87GSC%E9%BB%9E%E6%93%8A%E7%9A%84%E7%9B%B8%E9%97%9C%E6%80%A7.png" width="861"/><figcaption>Correlation between "Number of Internal Links" and "GSC Clicks"</figcaption></figure>
<p>In addition, the more varied the linked anchor text of a web page is, the stronger the positive correlation is with clicks in GSC. This is somewhat related to the observation about sitewide links above, because there will only be one anchor text if the link appears in the template.</p>

<h2 id="toc-3">[Good article] Discussing the thumbnails of Google search (Thumbnails)</h2>
<p><a href="https://twitter.com/brodieseo/">Brodie Clark</a>This article studies the web thumbnails (thumbnails) on Google search very carefully. The so-called thumbnails refer to the following, which may have a row of pictures or a single picture. This thumbnail has been available on mobile phones for a while, but has only started to appear in large numbers on desktop searches in the past year.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 58 – 2022.06.01~2022.06.15" class="wp-image-3654" decoding="async" height="331" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2022/06/image-3.png" width="552"/><figcaption>There are three situations in which a web page has a row of thumbnails, one thumbnail, and no thumbnails.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The article states that thumbnails appear in approximately 40% of desktop searches and 65% of mobile searches. In addition, Google will only allow up to four web pages to have multiple thumbnails on one search result.</p>
<p>In addition, regarding the appearance of "a row of thumbnails", the article provides three reasons to explain why the webpage does not have multiple thumbnails. The first is pure ranking. Google believes that other web pages are more worthy of having multiple thumbnails; the second is display reasons, you can site: the web page and add keywords to know whether the web page is qualified; the third is indexing issues, whether the images on the web page are not seen at all.</p>
<p>There are many observations in the article, including his list of eight reasons why it’s easier to have a multi-thumbnail appearance on a web page.</p>

<p>John was asked why the reports in GSC (such as "Index Coverage Report") of a website that has not changed anything still fluctuate?</p>
<p>John said that this is a very normal phenomenon. The system behind Google is very complex, and different data centers will not be synchronized at all times. Therefore, it is recommended to (1.) Pay special attention to the sudden rise or drop in the chart, which means that Google has encountered a new situation when crawling the website.</p>

<h2 id="toc-10">[Abstract] Podcast - Web 3.0</h2>
<figure class="embed embed--youtube"><div class="embed__frame"><iframe allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen="" loading="lazy" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/Lz9KijaUrNg" title="YouTube video player"></iframe></div><figcaption><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lz9KijaUrNg" rel="noopener noreferrer">View original video on YouTube</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In this episode, several people get together to discuss Web 3.0, a next-generation online world that is gradually being discussed with the blockchain craze. The contents are only their personal opinions and are not Google’s official position. Whether you are a novice who is not familiar with this concept or someone who has already stepped into the world of blockchain, I highly recommend listening to this podcast episode. It talks about the web3 concepts they know from the simplest to the more profound, and it does not always mention the benefits of web3 like the articles in the chain circle.</p>
<p>First of all, of course, define Web1 and Web2. Web1 refers to the earliest Internet. It is not easy to store and update physical data, so it is put on the Internet so that everyone can browse it directly, which is considered Read Only. Next is Web2, where people can communicate with others through social platforms, or easily set up their own website or upload content to platforms such as Pixnet and YouTube, which is considered Read &amp; Write.</p>
<p>Finally, there is Web3. We no longer want to be kidnapped by the platform, and we do not want our content to lose value due to changes in the platform's policies or algorithms, but we want to truly "own" the content.</p>
<p>The three also discussed content placed in the Walled Garden (content behind a paid or subscription wall) and why more and more creators do not want content to be absorbed for free. Martin believes that this is all related to the exchange of value. The owner of a business will be willing to put content on Facebook, because in addition to adding content to Facebook, it also provides a channel for him to be found, and both parties gain value; but for creators, the value to Facebook (platform users seeing good content) may be far greater than the value to the creator (a few likes + a sense of accomplishment) when posting valuable works to Facebook.</p>
