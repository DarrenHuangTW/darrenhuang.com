<h2 id="toc-1">[News] December Product Review Algorithm Update</h2>
<p>"Product Review Algorithm" in<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-30.html#toc-2">Early April this year</a>debuted with the purpose of combating low-quality industry captions that only put basic or repetitive information with shopping guide links. After more than half a year, Google has made another adjustment to this algorithm, which was launched on December 1, affecting English content, and is expected to be updated within three weeks.</p>
<p>That time in April<a href="https://www.seroundtable.com/google-product-reviews-update-big-31235.html">has a significant impact</a>, I myself have witnessed ~20% fluctuations in the traffic of several websites. If you manage this review or shopping guide website, you can pay close attention to the recent fluctuations. Two new principles have been added to this announcement:</p>
<ol><li>Provide evidence of your own actual experience with the product (visual, auditory, etc.) to support your expertise and reinforce the authenticity of your review.</li><li>Putting links to multiple sellers in your reviews gives readers the option to buy from the merchant of their choice.</li></ol>
<p>The second point caused some concern on Twitter<a href="https://twitter.com/ianhowells/status/1466132487340441603">Discussion</a>, because Amazon's terms of use state that reviewers cannot use Amazon's product information (pictures, videos, product descriptions...), but link to non-Amazon websites. In response, Google’s official representative<a href="https://twitter.com/akent99/status/1466177137640030209">only responds</a>It is up to the commenter to choose which one to comply with.</p>

<h2 id="toc-2">[News] Google Merchant Launches Three New Competitive Exposure Metrics</h2>
<p>Google Merchant Center allows merchants to set up Google Shopping ads or place<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-17.html#toc-1">Product free listing</a>on Google Shopping platform. Recently, a new competitive visibility report was launched on the platform, which contains three very useful indicators.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Relative visibility</strong>- is a competitor product, relative to the percentage of impressions your product has on Google Shopping.</li><li><strong>Page overlap rate</strong>- refers to the overlap rate when your products appear on Google Shopping, and the products of competing merchants are also exposed at the same time.</li><li><strong>Sorting lag rate (Higher position rate)</strong>- Represents the percentage of your competitor ranking in the front when both you and your competitor have products.</li></ul>

<h2 id="toc-6">[Research] Are backlinks still important for SEO?</h2>
<p>In the SEO circle, you often hear people saying that links are no longer important, so in the spirit of experimentation, Ahrefs did this test to check it out.<a href="./what-is-backlink-in-seo.html">Backlink
Does</a>still have any impact on website rankings? The method they used was not to add a large number of backlinks and then observe the rankings (if there was a way to do this, the quality of the links must not be high), but to select three articles and put a total of 3,476 links into the disavow file, asking Google not to count the value of these links.</p>
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Illustration for SEO e-Newsletter Issue 46 – 2021.12.01~2021.12.15" class="wp-image-3203" decoding="async" height="439" loading="lazy" src="./wp-content/uploads/2021/12/%E5%8F%8D%E5%90%91%E9%80%A3%E7%B5%90%E7%9A%84SEO%E5%BD%B1%E9%9F%BF-975x1024.png" width="418"/><figcaption>Changes in web traffic before and after adding/removing disavowed links</figcaption></figure></div>
<p>The conclusion is that links still have a considerable impact on rankings. Another interesting point is that after removing the disavow file, the ranking of the webpage does not necessarily return to the original level. The reason is not sure, but I personally guess that during the period of ranking drop, Google saw that the competing webpage that replaced it could also meet the searcher's intention, so it continued to rank the competing webpage at the top.</p>

<h2 id="toc-10">[Abstract] Google Search Podcast – Inclusive Language (Inclusive Language)</h2>
<p>Inclusive language is a topic that has become popular in recent years. It basically advocates the use of neutral words when speaking or writing to avoid the inherent stereotypes of language. For example, in inclusive writing you should use fireperson (neutral) rather than fireman (masculine) or firewoman (feminine). When I saw the title of this podcast, to be honest, my immediate reaction was to say, does SEO matter now? But after listening to it, I was very enlightened, because I heard people discussing this topic more and more often in the company or on the Internet.</p>
<p>Although the concept of using neutral words is very simple, there are many difficulties in implementing it in practice. For example, "student" in French is divided into<code>étudiant (陽性)</code>or<code>étudiante (陰性)</code>, neutral words do not exist, so they need to be created and become<code>étudiant/e</code>or<code>étudiant·e</code>, and the plural type becomes<code>étudiant/es</code>or<code>étudiant·e·s</code>. Although there is a more common neutral writing style in writing, how to pronounce Google Voice Assistant is also a problem. If we extend this issue to other complex languages, or even inclusivity beyond gender, we can have a little idea of ​​how broad the entire issue is.</p>
<p>John mentioned the challenges that Google will encounter when searching. There are three main stages.<strong>(1.)</strong>is in<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-27.html#toc-8">index stage</a>When you see neutral words (or any new words) used in the text, you must be able to extract them;<strong>(2.)</strong>in<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-32-33.html#toc-10">ranking stage</a>, the algorithm must match the search term with its neutral version (or vice versa) when understanding the search term;<strong>(3.)</strong>Finally, it is on the knowledge graph (knowledge graph), such as "Who is the first lady of the United States?", the original<code>第一夫人</code>is the relationship between two entities (node or entity). This relationship will need to change due to the use of neutral language.</p>
<p>In summary, they encourage everyone to use inclusive language, especially if you/your readers care about it. Google searches will encounter various challenges, such as the ones mentioned above, and for example, when searching for firefighters, all images that come up are male-biased. However, this is a problem that Google needs to solve, and it is not something that content creators need to worry about because of SEO.</p>
