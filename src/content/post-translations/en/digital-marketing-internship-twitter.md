<h3><span>Twitter</span></h3>
</div>
<div><span>Managing Twitter is interesting and challenging, because firstly, I am not that familiar with it, and secondly, followers on Twitter come and go quickly. If you post too frequently or are too boring, people will unfollow you. I also use Twitter personally, and the page will be washed out very quickly, so if I see Tweets that have nothing to do with me, or if I see an account that I have forgotten why I follow it and often posts things that interest me, I will immediately want to unfollow him.</span></div>
<div><span> </span></div>
<div><span>Facebook doesn't have this problem. If fans find your content boring, they usually won't click on it. If they don't click on it, they won't like it. If there is no interaction, fewer and fewer people will appear on the graffiti wall, so the number of people won't rise and fall as dramatically as on Twitter.</span></div>
<div><span>
</span><span>During my internship (09/14-12/10), the number of Followers increased from 834 to 950. Although it did not increase by a lot, it fortunately did not decrease. I will record some of my experience in operating Twitter~</span>

<h4><span>5. Who is unfollowing me?</span></h4>
<div><span>As mentioned at the beginning, if the content on Twitter is boring, it will be easily unfollowed. Sometimes it will be very frustrating to lose 3 or 4 followers for no reason over the weekend. At this time, I will use<a href="http://unfollowers.com/">unfollowers.com</a>Let’s see who are the people who unfollowed me.</span></div>
<div><span> </span></div>
<div><span>Some people on Twitter will first follow a bunch of accounts in order to get others' attention. The followed people will receive notifications and sometimes "follow back", which is a bit like mutual likes on Facebook, competing for each other's popularity.</span></div>
<div><span> </span></div>
<div><span>So you can use this tool to see who is unfollowing you. If the unfollowers are all people who have a lot of followers and a lot of following, then it is probably the above situation, and you don’t have to blame yourself for your poor performance~</span></div>
<div><span> </span></div>
<h4><span>6. Following/Followers ratio</span></h4>
<div><span>A healthy Twitter account should have many people following it, but it follows very few people. If the number of followers and followers is tens of thousands like the one above, it looks super "spammy". The boss didn't know how to make this ratio greater than 1 (we follow more people than people who follow us), so I also continue to unfollow some people.</span></div>
<div><span> </span></div>
<div><span>Another useful feature of the website mentioned in point 5 above is that you can check the accounts you follow to see which ones have not been on Twitter for several months and unfollow them all. In fact, I personally think that 80% can be deleted, otherwise NGIN now has more than 1,400 people following it, and they would not read the Dynamic Times at all.</span></div>
