Do not edit by hand. -->have you ever asked<b>"Why does an image I put on the website not show up in the image results when others search for it?"</b>, most people should not have this problem... because as long as most images have undergone appropriate image optimization (image file name, size, Alt Tag, article content... etc.), even if they are not ranked very high, they should still appear in the search results when someone searches for related keywords.

This article is about what I am doing<a href="./seo-reputation-managment.html" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Reputation Management</a>A little theory I discovered at the time is that image search is the same as general web search. Google hopes that the information it provides has real value, so for "duplicate" and "similar" content, Google will decide which one to appear in the SERP based on other factors. Duplicate content that does not appear is not ranked lower, but almost disappears.<h3> Diversity Matters</h3>

<div style="text-align:left">The picture above is an article I recently read by Teacher Wu Tianyuan. If you use Exact Match to search for it, you will see a paragraph at the bottom, saying that Google has excluded other very similar (basically the same) results and only selected two for presentation.</div>
<div style="text-align:left"></div>
<div style="text-align:left">This is the same as Host Clustering. Host Clustering means that in most cases, even if the authority of a domain is very high, Google will only select 1~3 (*.) web pages from this domain to appear on the SERP. Unless Google feels that the search string is really consistent with the website, and other pages of the website can also provide valuable information.</div>
