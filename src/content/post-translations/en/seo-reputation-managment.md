(This article may be quite long, please forgive me)<h3>What is Reputation Management(RM)?</h3>RM should be a word that comes from PR. It helps a company and people create a good image, how to deal with negative news, etc. In the online world, if a piece of news appears that will harm the company's image and causes some repercussions, some people will share and write articles to respond to the news, and links will follow. This negative news may appear when someone searches for the company's name. If it appears on the first page of SERP, people who are not aware of it will know that this kind of thing has happened to this company.

Therefore, Reputation Management, in SEO, refers to "burying" this kind of negative news on the second and third pages, so that people who originally didn't know about it will still be less likely to know about it after searching.<div style="text-align:center"><a href="https://1.bp.blogspot.com/-Dvxj0IQV93U/VttCB-vIfaI/AAAAAAAAAso/4Ux3AKN9rt8/s1600/best-place-to-hide-a-dead-body-is-Google-page-2.gif" style="margin-left:1em;margin-right:1em"><img alt="Illustration for SEO work - Reputation Managment experience" decoding="async" height="212" loading="lazy" src="https://1.bp.blogspot.com/-Dvxj0IQV93U/VttCB-vIfaI/AAAAAAAAAso/4Ux3AKN9rt8/s400/best-place-to-hide-a-dead-body-is-Google-page-2.gif" width="400"/></a></div>

<h3>How to "bury" Negative Results</h3>
<div>It is impossible to make this news disappear completely unless you ask Google to take it down (the content violates the regulations) or contact the reporter of the article. What we can do is<b>Create other web pages, and then let these web pages outrank the article on the keyword "Peter Merrigan"</b>。</div>
<div></div>These web pages used to crowd out negative results can be<b>real website</b>, I helped the CEO create his own www.petermerrigan.com. The keyword is in the URL, and the content inside will also contain information related to the CEO.
