<p>API contains four blocks: (1.)<code>indexStatusResult</code>- Displays the inclusion status of web pages, (2.)<code>ampResult</code>- Displays whether the AMP version of the web page has errors, (3.)<code>mobileUsabilityResult</code>- Displays whether the webpage is suitable for browsing on mobile devices, (4.)<code>richResultsResult</code>- The page contains compound search results supported by Google.</p>

<h2 id="toc-7">[Good article] Three linking techniques of black hat SEO</h2>
<p>Of course, I don’t recommend everyone to touch black hat stuff, but occasionally it’s helpful to know these techniques, which can help you avoid becoming a victim. In this article, the author talks about 3 common black hat SEO methods.</p>
<p>The first one is a method commonly used by companies that specialize in selling external links, providing links from high domain authority (DA) domains. DA is an indicator that many people use to measure the value of external links, but this informal SEO indicator launched by Moz is very easy to manipulate. The following figure is an example,<strong>I can easily make a link from google.com to my website</strong>, if you do a few more and find ways to let crawlers (especially crawlers of third-party analysis tools) know, the DA of darrenhuang.com will increase accordingly.</p>

<h2 id="toc-8">[Official] How Google Maps Reviews Work</h2>
<p>Google published this article to give you a glimpse into the review and operation behind Google Maps reviews. It is difficult for this type of official public articles to have any information that is too secret, but the general direction is to rely on machine learning to scale up + manual review to provide training data to review millions of comments every day.</p>
<p>After pressing Submit, a review will first go through a round of machine review. If it passes, it will be posted within a few seconds. However, there will be other algorithms and human reviews later to ensure that there are no other abnormalities in the review. These abnormalities include but are not limited to: "A merchant has received a large number of one-star or five-star reviews in a short period of time", "Whether the merchant has been exposed on social media or in the news", "whether the reviews of the same merchant come from the same batch of accounts"...etc.</p>
<p>In addition, Google will also take further protective measures for upcoming events and locations (such as elections) to prevent comments from being abused.</p>

<h2 id="toc-9">[Official] AI in Google Search</h2>
<p>The author of this article is Pandu Nayak (VP of Google search department). He talks about how Google search went from exact text matching in the early days to later<a href="./seo-newsletter-issue-8.html#toc-7">Typo correction</a>mechanism, and now more complex AI algorithms continue to optimize search results. In the article, Pandu briefly discussed four of Google’s most representative AI systems in recent years.</p>
<ul><li><strong>RankBrain </strong>- Launched in 2015, it is the first deep learning* system applied to search, allowing Google to connect text and concepts. For example: "<em>what’s the title of the consumer at the highest level of a food chain</em>"。</li><li><strong>Neural Matching</strong>- Launched in 2018, allows Google to understand the concept behind a vague search term or web page. For example: "<em>insights how to manage a green</em>"。</li><li><strong>BERT </strong>- Launched in 2019, it allows Google to learn that different combinations of words represent different meanings and intentions. For example: "<em>can you get medicine for someone pharmacy</em> "。</li><li><strong>MUM </strong>- Launched in 2021, allowing Google to understand the information behind it across languages and formats (ex: text, images),<strong>is different from the other three and has not yet been used to optimize search results.</strong>. For example: Facing a picture of a floral shirt, enter a text "<em>Found socks with this pattern
Search example for</em>」。</li></ul>

<h2 id="toc-10">[Abstract] Google Search Podcast – Designing a new search engine Steve</h2>
<p>In this episode of the podcast, the trio discusses a hypothetical question. If a new search engine were to be designed today, how would they communicate with webmasters about how to improve website rankings. This is actually the usual job responsibilities of the three of them, they just use a fake search engine name Steve to prevent people from taking it too seriously. They believe that the purpose of search engines is very clear, but "external communication" is difficult because it can easily be misinterpreted or misunderstood.</p>
<p>For example, everyone will intuitively think<a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/advanced/guidelines/hidden-text-links">hidden text</a>is not good for SEO, but although the text hidden behind the tab is a reasonable usage situation, it is often misunderstood by the outside world as being harmful to SEO. Just like interpreting the letter of the law, Google's guidelines list things they do and don't want to see sites do, and they try to define them, but not everything is black and white and there are always gray areas that can be over-interpreted.</p>
<p>Another point is that most of Google's suggestions are best practices rather than guidelines, such as whether the web page is secure (HTTPS), whether the HTML is compliant, whether the image has alt text, etc. These are all bonus points, and they will not be completely excluded just because you do one less thing.</p>
<p>For example<a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/advanced/guidelines/google-images">Google Guide</a>states that pictures should have alt text. Many people even put alt text on decorative pictures and add keywords, but this is not necessary and is not good for the website, readers, or search engines. (Digital Engine was also on FB<a href="https://www.facebook.com/searchenginecommunity/posts/319885068749442">Discussion</a>has talked about similar topics)</p>
